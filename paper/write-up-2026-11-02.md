## 1. Referee and common-row objective

Alice and Bob receive independent fair binary strings and each selects one of their own coordinates after seeing the other's string. They win if both selected bits are one. Let V₂ be the supremum of finite-stack success probabilities.

In coefficient dimension d, use m_d=2(2ᵈ−1) labels (a,b), with a≠0 and b∈F₂. For a partition P of these labels, independently retain each block with probability 1/2. Let U be the union of retained blocks and define

\[
H_d(P)=\frac1{m_d}\mathbb E\max_{x\in\mathbb F_2^d}
\#\{(a,b)\in U:a\cdot x\oplus b=1\}.
\]

For every d, V₂≤max_P H_d(P). Choose a uniform hidden label J=(a,b), independent uniform coefficient rows R_i, and Bob's fair string Z. Alice's bits a·R_i⊕b are independent and fair for every fixed J, so the original input law is preserved. Reveal R to both players and J to Bob. For fixed R, Bob's choices partition the labels according to the selected coordinate of Z. Those distinct coordinates are independent fair block-retention bits. Alice chooses one row; allowing every x∈F₂ᵈ only helps her. Her success probability is bounded by H_d(P).

For a three-dimensional coefficient frame W, the corresponding local objective is

\[
F_W(P|_W)=\frac1{14}\mathbb E\max_x\#\{(a,b)\in U:a\in W\setminus\{0\},\ a\cdot x\oplus b=1\}.
\]

Each label appears in the same number of frames. Taking a maximum after averaging cannot exceed averaging the separate maxima, so

\[
H_d(P)\le\frac1{N_d}\sum_W F_W(P|_W),\qquad
N_d=\frac{(2^d-1)(2^{d-1}-1)(2^{d-2}-1)}{21}.
\]

The same argument for hyperplanes T gives

\[
H_d(P)\le\frac1{2^d-1}\sum_T H_{d-1}(P|_T).
\tag{1}
\]

All expectations use the same retained global blocks. Their restrictions have exactly the required local fair-block law; independence between different frames is not assumed.

## 6. A stronger bound by retaining the exceptional penalty

For exceptional family f, let r_f be its minimum R₄ and s_f its maximum H₄. The complete table gives

\[
c_4=\min_f\frac{896N_4(B-s_f)-r_f}{40-r_f}=\frac{41}{8}.
\]

This proves the pointwise bound

\[
H_4(P)\le B-\frac{R_4(P)+c_4(40-R_4(P))_+}{896N_4}.
\tag{4}
\]

For R₄≥40 this is (2). For R₄<40, its exceptional family's score supplies the stated penalty; the audit checks the inequality at every relevant integer loss. In particular, the actual minimum 28 is preserved.

Set L₄=40. Assume the analogous inequality in dimension d−1 with L_(d−1), c_(d−1). There are h_d=2ᵈ−1 hyperplanes, and every three-frame belongs to i_d=2^(d−3)−1 of them. Therefore

\[
\sum_T R_{d-1}(P|_T)=i_dR_d(P),\qquad h_dN_{d-1}=i_dN_d.
\]

Averaging (4) with (1), and using that the sum of positive parts bounds the positive part of the sum, gives

\[
H_d(P)\le B-\frac{R_d(P)+c_{d-1}(t_d-R_d(P))_+}{896N_d},
\qquad t_d=\frac{h_dL_{d-1}}{i_d}.
\]

Put

\[
L_d=\lceil t_d\rceil,\qquad c_d=c_{d-1}(t_d-L_d+1).
\]

For integer r<L_d, t_d−r≥(t_d−L_d+1)(L_d−r), so the same penalized inequality holds with L_d,c_d. When c_d≥1, it implies the uniform bound B−L_d/(896N_d).

| d | Effective threshold L_d | c_d | Game upper bound |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 40 | 41/8 | 241/672 |
| 5 | 414 | 41/24 | 24903/69440 |
| 6 | 3726 | 41/24 | 24903/69440 |
| 7 | 31547 | 41/30 | 3795217/10582656 |

At dimension seven this is the displayed main bound. In dimension eight the coefficient falls below one, so this argument does not claim the next rounded threshold. All arithmetic is exact. The effective thresholds bound the common-row objective; they do **not** assert R_d≥L_d.

An independent geometric check constructs all 155 three-frames and 31 hyperplanes in F₂⁵, verifies every incidence multiplicity, and tests pointwise averaging over every retained-block state of two embedded partitions, one containing the loss-28 witness.
